## 4. Observational equivalence under anonymous experiments
Exhibit a fixed selector common to the two states: ABCD for theta<=.5, AC for .5<theta<=1, A for 1<theta<=2, empty for theta>2. This is an admissible pure NE for every real theta; each realized labeled share vector agrees (four .25; A/C .5 each; A 1; zero). Profit, aggregate share and HHI contain no additional differentiating signal. For any adaptive deterministic or randomized experiment protocol, couple the government's own random seed across states. Same starting information, same action conditional on equal prior history, same selected observation, hence induction equal histories and terminal action distribution, including stopping decisions. Existence of selector suffices for worst-case/uniform-identification impossibility, not every selector or identical equilibrium sets. If all-support selection produces triples with positive probability at .5, repeated independent observations can identify these two states in the limit; no finite certainty guaranteed. Fixed selection and fresh static experiments are essential, not a model of irreversible exits or informational signalling. Illustrative diagnostic: a myopic deletion rule starting from all firms, dropping a strict-loss firm in fixed priority B,D,C,A, generates the shared path (retain at indifference); it is not imposed as the unique selector or a new dynamic model.

## 5. Exact worst-case policy regret under pure selectors
Regret C_z(S(theta))- min_S C_z(S). Same comparator attainable by pure-selection-robust anonymous thresholds in each state. Under shared selection, singleton/empty dominated by opposite pair in both states. Low theta (full) gives losses (0,7/400); middle (opposite pair) gives (9/400,0). Higher thresholds cannot improve on middle. Thus every deterministic experiment/terminal strategy worst-case loss>=min(9/400,7/400)=7/400. Selecting theta=.49 attains this under all pure selectors. If randomized, common path implies same distribution of decisions, p=probability of full via low action; replace any dominated higher action by pair for a lower bound. Regret >=max((9/400)(1-p),(7/400)p); equalizer p=9/16, regret=63/6400. Attained by choosing thresholds .49 (prob.9/16) and .525 (prob.7/16); these thresholds guarantee the desired cost under every pure selector. This is the exact minimax value for specifically {U,C}, all admissible pure selectors, randomized terminal scalar policy, free fresh experiments. For arbitrary larger state space it is only a lower-bound construction if both worlds admissible. No claim for mixed selection upper bound or arbitrary geometry. Arithmetic is existing Manski binary-action minimax-regret formula, not independent novelty.

## 6. Scope attacks / parameter audit
Symmetric independent mixed NE exists for both states for .5<theta<2: conditional expected share given activation f(x)=1-3x/2+x^2-x^3/4, derived by summing each of 8 other-player profiles for each firm. f strictly decreases on [0,1] from 1 to .25. Unique symmetric interior x solves f(x)=theta/2 (not unique NE overall); positive probability of inefficient outcomes including empty. Therefore pure robust target at .525 is NOT robust over all Nash equilibria. Same mixing propensity does NOT imply identical realized sales; triples reveal geometry. If admissible class includes pure and mixed, shared pure path still yields lower bound, not sharp mixed minimax value.
Antipodal family z=(0,a,1/2,1/2+a), 0<a<1/2: all-4 shares .25, same common path; T2=t/8 (best opposite pair), T3=t*(a^2+(.5-a)^2+.25)/4, T4=t*(a^2+(.5-a)^2)/2. Adjacent pair cannot beat opposite pair; differences T2-T3=T3-T4=t*a*(.5-a)/2 checked symbolically. For F/t<1/8, and nonempty coverage/benchmark, cutoff F/t=a*(.5-a)/2 gives 4 below, 2 above (3 tied only at boundary); for U vs C strict count mismatch when 9/800<F/t<1/32. At high F one firm or empty may dominate; a=0,.5 coincident sites excluded. Positive interval within this symmetric family and fixed-cost domain, not open robust result in unrestricted geometry.
