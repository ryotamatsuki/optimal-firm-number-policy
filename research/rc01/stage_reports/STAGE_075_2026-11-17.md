## 1. Mechanism and assumptions
Phenomenon: different product differentiation despite identical sales. Friction: regulator sees only exact labeled equilibrium quantities and can vary an anonymous participation incentive, not individual firms or latent distances. Strategic response: the least viable sites may exit under a common tighter threshold; surviving firms receive redistributed demand. Equilibrium effect: for known cyclic order and positive gaps, odd N full shares identify geometry; even N nonuniform full shares permit finite adaptive exact queries for all pure selectors; equal shares permit a common observationally equivalent selection path. Welfare: opposite robust optimal common policies in two admissible worlds under P3's strictly positive F/t interval. Empirical implication conditional on primitives: shares alone need not measure product substitution; endogenous assortment changes may reveal it. No claim equal shares are universally desirable or unidentified.
Essential for certified theorem: known directed circular order, positive ordered gaps, uniform consumer density, nearest-site fixed-price demand, common payoff margin and fixed cost, exact quantities, freely adjustable real common threshold with initialization, fresh static participation games, pure Nash, realized unknown selection; welfare additionally linear transport loss, neutral fiscal accounting and comparable costs. Objection: a theorem proving nonidentification exactly at a symmetry locus and recovering identification immediately outside it may be a useful diagnostic but does not yet convincingly establish a consequential policy failure; exact and reversible experiments are a generous information technology. The paper should show a reason why policy resolution rather than a purely mathematical degeneracy matters. Quantifying regret only on symmetric examples is not enough for this broader claim. This burden is assigned to a single narrowly falsifiable question rather than adding unrelated mechanisms. Actor: AI analytic editorial challenge, with public article excerpts and prior proof records, no independent human reviewer, author adoption pending. 
Editor-facing pitch: In many differentiated-product markets the amount of business won by each firm is easier to observe than the consumer substitution patterns that matter for welfare. R-C01 shows that a policy instrument can also be an information-generating device, because a change in the participation incentive reshapes the assortment consumers face. The informative variation is generated strategically rather than assumed to be externally available. Within an ordered circular market, the model yields a precise positive identification result under arbitrary pure equilibrium selection and a sharp symmetry exception in which market histories admit observationally equivalent geometries. These geometries can entail conflicting robust welfare decisions even with identical observed sales. That is a real theoretical contribution, not a new general method of partial identification. The current concern is whether its negative welfare implication remains economically important when a policy maker cannot finely tune that common incentive. A bounded resolution test directly addresses that concern. The baseline is not rejected as mathematics and could support a focused diagnostic note.
