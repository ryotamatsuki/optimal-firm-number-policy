## 5. Economic contribution / editor-facing case (English, 152 words)
A regulator may observe precisely how many firms serve a market and their individual sales while having little knowledge of the differentiation structure relevant to consumer welfare. Conventional partial-identification methods describe sets of compatible market primitives, but that observation alone does not tell a regulator when its existing policy lever can generate informative variation. In this tightly specified fixed-price circular market, anonymous participation incentives provide such variation except at a sharply characterized symmetry boundary. The positive result is constructive and robust to arbitrary pure equilibrium selection. The negative result establishes that equal market shares need not imply equal differentiation and that a common observable policy-response history can coexist with opposed robust welfare objectives. This makes the composition of observed market participation, rather than merely the number of observations, directly relevant to policy learning. The results are theoretical and deliberately restricted: they do not estimate real-market losses or imply that regulators lack other useful information.

Skeptical editor case: known ordered circle and exact share equations supply unusually strong measurements; the hidden dimension is one scalar, symmetry is a codimension N-2 locus, and low-cost near-threshold experiments assume reversible static participation and arbitrary taxes. Much of the proof uses elementary geometric algebra and standard partial identification. A full paper may therefore be too stylized even if the theorem is distinct. The positive identification protocol and explicit welfare conflict provide more than a counterexample, but Stage7 must assess economic interpretation and quantify stakes without recasting a knife-edge as generic.
