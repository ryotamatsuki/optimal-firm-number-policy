## Phenomenon versus hypothesis
Constructed thought experiment (not observed real markets): U=(0,.25,.5,.75) and C=(0,.05,.5,.55) on unit circle, t=1, F=.02, v-c=2, positive common margin, fixed common price and full coverage, common active transfer s real. All four active, each labeled demand 1/4. Welfare-optimal fixed-site subsets differ (4 in U, 2 in C). One common pure-equilibrium selection function yields identical labeled activity and sales at every common policy threshold theta=2(F-s)/m.
Hypothesis: equilibrium participation information generated by anonymous policy cannot always distinguish latent substitution geometry relevant for social resource cost. Not a claim of equality of entire equilibrium correspondences or universal impossibility. Not empirically observed. Assumptions and alternative channels to audit.

## Actors and frictions
Government chooses anonymous scalar transfer and terminal policy; observes labeled activity, sales, known F,m,t; not latent locations/gaps or mismatch losses. Firms know the relevant market primitives and simultaneously choose activity; consumers choose nearest available product at common fixed price. Fresh static game each experimental round, no persistence, no strategic intertemporal signaling; pure Nash selection unknown, may be a fixed selection function. Government observes realized equilibrium only, not every alternative equilibrium, and cannot force identity-specific activity in baseline. This specifies provisional question, not a full model. Terminal-policy regret must be distinguished from fixed-site planner cost and own-state restricted-instrument optimum; audit whether same benchmark is attainable in each state for contemplated selection class. Policy experiment costs ignored in preliminary example: impossibility with costless permitted experiments would be stronger than cost-based difficulty. No claim under observed positions or endogenous pricing.

## Mechanism register (finite intake: five distinct mechanisms, two targeted public source-search batches, one exact consistency check; no further model development)
M1 (primary): endogenous observational equivalence of participation responses under anonymous experiments. Contribution candidate: construct two economically distinct states, all common-policy histories possibly indistinguishable, different welfare-optimal actions; establish exact information/selection boundary. Advance to audit.
M2: geometry inversion / rank deficiency (known cyclic order: q=(I+P)d/2; even n alternating null vector, odd n invertible). Ancillary algebraic lemma or diagnostic, not standalone novelty. Audit unknown order and full recovery domain.
M3: equilibrium selection as information channel. Competing explanation: common selection hides differences but full-support repeat selection or full correspondence observed may identify; do not assume uniform impossibility. Audit credible stable/dynamic selection restrictions without adding them to baseline.
M4: targeted product-removal observation or an observed local gap restores decision-relevant information. Candidate comparison benchmark; targeted removal is known experimental method, not a new concept. Investigate whether minimal observation suffices for policy choice; defer actual targeted instrument design.
M5: unknown utility/transport valuations rather than geometry. With fixed shares and price, welfare may be unobservable even if geometry known; generic information-gap explanation, do not add new unknowns to this study. Hold separate to distinguish structural contribution from definitionally missing welfare.

## Strongest prior-art objection and application-neutral fingerprint
Parent: partial identification/statistical decision theory (Manski 2025, Identification and Statistical Decision Theory, Econometric Theory 41(4) 977–993; arXiv 2022 revised 2024 abstract https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11318 and published bibliography https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/etheor/v41y2025i4p977-993_7.html; full theorem not read). Closest IO parent: Berry & Haile 2014, Identification in Differentiated Products Markets Using Market Level Data, https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA9027 (publisher abstract; identifies demand/welfare under IV and exclusion/support conditions). Sector-neutral fingerprint: latent state z, scalar anonymous action a, endogenous equilibrium correspondence E(z,a), realized signal o=O(z,S,a), S in E(z,a); terminal welfare w(z,S) and a decision rule mapping adaptive histories to actions. If common selection produces same observations for all allowed actions but best actions differ, generic partial-ID decision problem yields nonidentification and positive regret. Therefore observational equivalence -> regret is a known decision-theoretic argument; do not claim general novelty. Remaining candidate economic learning is constructive characterization of which endogenous market interventions generate welfare-relevant identifying variation, separation by selection/geometry, and minimal measurement/experiment within economically defensible restrictions. Risk: known identification theorem or simple rank deficiency may exhaust this. Absorption UNRESOLVED. Stage 1/2 must inspect actual theorem domains, map primitives and prove or reject distinct residual.

## Falsifiable one-sentence question
In a differentiated-product participation market where the government observes labeled sales and active sets but not latent substitution geometry, can adaptive anonymous transfer experiments identify the welfare-optimal feasible policy under an explicit class of equilibrium selectors, and what minimal additional observation separates observationally equivalent markets?
